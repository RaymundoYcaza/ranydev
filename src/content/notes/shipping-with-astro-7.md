It's been a long, long time since I used Drupal, Joomla, Xoops, Nuke, or
WordPress.

And, sure, it's been even longer since those old attempts at building blogs in
HTML and trying to impress myself with Dreamweaver.

I don't know about you, but back then I actually liked the idea of hand-coding
websites for my blogs.

But of course, problems would eventually show up: tedious, repetitive work,
never enough time to automate things, comments, and a whole lot more that made
the idea of building static sites less appealing.

There was also a middle ground: build something with PHP or another interpreted
language to make it a bit more dynamic without losing the simplicity.

But then other issues would come up and things would get tricky.

You know what I mean, right?

At least in my case, over time I forgot about building a blog with something
simple like HTML, CSS, and JavaScript.

WordPress was what I settled for, guiltily, and I gave in to the uncomfortable
comfort of dealing with plugin updates, database backups, optimizations,
version upgrade problems, hosting costs, and a whole lot more.

We know how to code, sure. But wouldn't it be nice to write things in a much
simpler way? Something closer to grabbing a notebook and a pencil, writing a
page, and pinning it to some random hallway bulletin board?

So just when we thought we'd found the sweet spot, or maybe we'd only found a
way to stay stuck, along came what everyone calls the disruptive phenomenon of
artificial intelligence.

Yeah, I know AI had been around for a long time, and I know we're talking about
generative AI. You know that, and I know that. We're just having a relaxed chat
here, right?

Anyway, like I was saying, AI showed up alongside the aggressive marketing
campaign pushing it, and it's stirred up some chaos and confusion.

And yet, in the middle of all that chaos, there are opportunities, like a fire
in the forest.

Using this tool let me dust off that old itch to have a simple site I could
make as simple or as fancy as I wanted, without spending much time on it.

And sure, I could build it with plain HTML and add the occasional dynamic bit
when I needed one.

But instead, I sat down with my cup of coffee and my old ThinkPad and built
this blog, shiny new logo and all, in just a couple of hours. (Yeah, free LLMs
aren't that fast, but why am I in a hurry?)

Now here I am, writing on a Sunday night like the old days, without thinking
about likes or views, and without adding Google Analytics.

I think we should always look for some kind of balance between what works and
what we actually need.

Why build a whole Symfony framework for a simple blog?

And why do I need to prove I can build an award-winning blog with nothing but
my pocketknife and a toothbrush?

The right solution is hard to find, but it's always somewhere between pride
and being way too comfortable.

So while AI is making our star collapse into a black hole and everyone is
pulling their hair out, I've decided to step through that black hole's portal
and travel back in time. And you have no idea how much I'm enjoying it!

See you.
